WebMCP-Project-GodreryTone-Jewellery-Shopify-Storefront-Testing-Project

I applied the WebMCP implementation to my on-establishment shopify storefront for real products integration to improve customization through AI agent. This project serves as a great achievement in my building career. This is following the new skills I have gained and look forward to apply in many sectors I will be working on in future.

GodreryTone Jewellery Store — WebMCP

Project Overview

My project is based on a real-world Shopify store experience.

👉 Click here to view the GodreryTone Jewellery Store-https://godrerytonejewellery.myshopify.com/

To my surprise, this WebMCP opportunity would fit so closely with something I was already thinking about while working toward making GodreryTone Jewellery a fully functional online store.

GodreryTone Jewellery is currently in the building stage and is run and managed by Collins Mogire. The store offers jewellery products by connecting with many suppliers around the globe and connecting them to a global customer reach, considering the regions and countries that each supplier is able to supply their products to.

In the business world, one of the main concerns of any entrepreneur is answering one question:

How can I improve the customer experience?

That question became particularly important when I started thinking about what happens when a real customer arrives with a very specific need.

They might know the occasion, who they are buying for, the style they want, their budget, and the country they are shopping from — but they may not know which products in the store actually satisfy all of those requirements.

The Customer Questions I Was Already Thinking About

User and customer preferences have always been important, especially when shopping online:

«"I want this product. How can I get it as quickly as possible?"»

«"I don't want to stay in the search bar all the time searching for a product that matches my preference. Where can I find the product that fits my preference as quickly as possible? There are too many collections here with too many products. I don't think I can do this!"»

«"I see these products appearing when I switch to another country. Can I get this product since I don't see it in my country? Can it be shipped since the country it happens to appear under is my neighbour and I can pick it from there? Who can explain to the customer why the same product is appearing in the neighbouring country with another price, maybe cheaper or more than the price in the customer's country?"»

«"And if we introduce an AI agent, can this agent direct me straight to the product page instead of me going to the page and starting the search again?"»

These were not theoretical questions for me.

They were questions connected to the actual process of building the store and thinking about how customers would eventually interact with it.

Then I Encountered WebMCP

An encounter with WebMCP was a game changer.

I realized that this opportunity could actually help me address the exact customer-experience problems I was already thinking about.

That is what led to this project.

The Real Store Behind the Project

GodreryTone Jewellery is not a fictional catalogue created only for this challenge.

It is a real Shopify jewellery store currently being developed toward becoming a fully functional online store.

The existing Shopify infrastructure already provides the store's core commerce foundation:

- Products
- Collections
- Product information
- Pricing
- Market configuration
- Storefront functionality
- Purchasing infrastructure

The purpose of this project is therefore not to replace Shopify.

Instead, WebMCP provides an additional intelligence and interaction layer that helps an AI agent work with the existing store.

The Real-World Challenge: Product Availability by Country

Operating a real jewellery store also introduces real-world constraints.

One of the most important is product supply by country.

Different suppliers may have different fulfilment capabilities.

A supplier may be able to supply a particular product to one country but not another.

Therefore, the question is not simply:

«"Does this product exist in my store?"»

It becomes:

«"Is this product appropriate and realistically available for this particular customer?"»

That distinction is important to the customer experience I wanted to build.

The Customer Experience I Wanted

Imagine a customer tells the store:

«"I'm shopping from Uganda. I need an elegant pair of earrings for my wife for a wedding, and I have a budget of UGX 100,000."»

A normal storefront can certainly display jewellery.

But there is much more information hidden inside that one request.

The system needs to understand:

- The customer's intent
- The jewellery they are looking for
- The occasion
- The recipient
- The desired style
- Relevant suitability considerations
- The customer's country
- Which products are relevant to that country
- Which products could satisfy the request
- The applicable product price
- Whether the intended product can actually be shipped
- Whether there are regional possibilities worth presenting
- How to take the customer directly to the relevant product

That is the experience I wanted to improve.

Country-Specific Product Availability

This is particularly important for GodreryTone because not every product necessarily has the same fulfilment possibility in every country.

The reason can be as practical as a supplier being unable to supply that destination.

A customer could potentially encounter a situation like this:

Product A

Available in the store

↓

Not currently serviceable for the customer's country

But there may be:

Product A / Regional Alternative

Potentially serviceable through another regional configuration

The WebMCP experience should not hide this complexity from the customer.

At the same time, it should not silently change the customer's country or shopping context.

Instead:

Identify → Explain → Present the option → Let the customer decide.

That is an important part of keeping the customer in control.

Faster Product Discovery

Another problem I wanted to address was how quickly a customer can get to something relevant.

A customer should not necessarily have to go through:

Open collection → Browse products → Open product → Go back → Try another collection → Filter → Repeat

when they have already described what they want.

Instead, the AI agent can understand the customer's request and use the WebMCP tools to narrow the possibilities.

For example:

«"Elegant wedding earrings for my wife, Uganda, within my budget."»

The result can be a focused set of potential products with the information needed to understand why they may fit.

And importantly, the customer can be given a direct link/path to the actual Shopify product page.

The AI helps with discovery.

Shopify remains the destination where the customer can inspect the product and continue shopping.

Where Currency Fits Into the Implementation

Currency is not one of the core customer problems that motivated this project.

There is a practical Shopify limitation in the current store: the subscription configuration limits the store's ability to freely operate with changed currencies.

The underlying store therefore continues to work with its USD pricing foundation.

However, we still wanted the AI shopping experience to be capable of handling a customer who expresses their budget in their own currency.

That is why the implementation includes manual conversion-rate infrastructure.

The idea is:

Customer's currency

↓

Conversion using the configured conversion rate

↓

USD budget for backend product matching

↓

Identify suitable products

↓

Convert the relevant price back for customer-facing presentation

This allows us to demonstrate the intended customer experience while respecting the actual limitations of the current Shopify subscription.

We are not presenting this as if Shopify already provides unrestricted multi-currency functionality for this store.

Instead, the manual conversion layer is a practical supplement that allows the WebMCP experience to support the intended flow today.

It is also a forward-looking capability.

As the store's Shopify capabilities evolve, this conversion layer can move toward the appropriate native or external currency infrastructure rather than remaining a permanent substitute.

WebMCP as a Supplement to Shopify

This is the central idea of the project.

I am not replacing Shopify.

I am supplementing it.

Shopify remains responsible for the actual store and its underlying commerce infrastructure.

WebMCP provides the AI agent with structured capabilities that help it work with that existing store in a way that is much closer to how a customer naturally expresses a shopping request.

The relationship is:

REAL SHOPIFY STORE

↓

Existing commerce foundation

↓

WebMCP agent-facing layer

↓

Natural-language customer request

↓

Structured shopping decisions

↓

Relevant products

↓

Market / availability considerations

↓

Shipping considerations

↓

Customer decision

↓

Direct product / purchase path

The difference is important.

This is not simply adding a chatbot that sits on top of the store.

The WebMCP tools represent specific capabilities corresponding to real parts of the shopping journey.

The 12 WebMCP Tools

The WebMCP implementation divides this experience into specialized tools:

#| Tool| Purpose
1| Shopping Intent| Understand what the customer is actually trying to buy
2| Occasion| Understand the occasion behind the purchase
3| Style| Interpret the customer's desired style
4| Recipient| Understand who the jewellery is intended for
5| Age| Handle relevant suitability information
6| Regional Market| Determine the customer's applicable shopping context
7| Budget & Currency| Normalize customer budget information for matching
8| Product / Catalogue| Find relevant products from current store data
9| Market-Aware Pricing| Present applicable pricing appropriately
10| Shipping Eligibility| Determine whether the intended purchase can proceed for the destination
11| Customer Purchase Decision| Keep important regional/purchase choices under customer control
12| Final Integration| Coordinate the verified shopping result and purchase path

The important point is that these tools are not twelve unrelated features.

Together, they represent the journey from:

«"This is what I want."»

to:

«"These are the products that could fit me, this is what I need to know about them, and this is where I can go to continue."»

What WebMCP Changed

The goal was never to make an AI that simply says:

«"Here are some jewellery products."»

The goal was to make the existing store more capable of responding to a real shopping request.

A customer can express their needs naturally.

The agent can break those needs into structured requirements.

The WebMCP tools can coordinate the relevant store information.

Country-specific availability can be considered.

Supplier limitations can be respected.

Budget information can be normalized.

Potential products can be identified faster.

Pricing can be presented appropriately.

Shipping can be considered before the customer is led too far.

Regional possibilities can be surfaced without silently making the decision.

And relevant products can lead the customer directly back into the normal Shopify experience.
